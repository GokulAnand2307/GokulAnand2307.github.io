A layoff headline is not a layoff count.


I kept coming back to that while reading recent company announcements.


On 17 November, Imperial Brands reported 2,100 exits confirmed or in consultation, plus a further 2,100–2,500 roles expected to go by the end of 2030.

That is a serious workforce change. It is also different from saying 4,600 people have already left.


For Gokul’s Data Signal 15, I went back to six companies’ own disclosures to understand the numbers behind the headlines.

Some report a percentage of their workforce. Others report a role count. Some describe consultations; others describe plans that run into 2027 or 2030.


The human impact matters. So does describing it accurately.


As a business analyst, I’d want to know what happens after the announcement: are people redeployed, does workload rise, and what happens to service quality and delivery?

Headcount savings alone won’t answer those questions.


When a company announces a restructuring, what evidence would convince you that it has improved the business beyond reducing costs?
