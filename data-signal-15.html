The question is not simply “who cut the most?” Amazon describes an organisational reduction; Accenture reports severance alongside net workforce growth; HSBC reports a net FTE decline. These are different measures. This is a comparison of selected disclosures, not a ranking of industries or a complete 2026 layoffs tracker.

Disclosed job-reduction components

Horizontal axis: roles, from zero to 20,000. Selected rounds and programme components; not completed exits or annual totals.

Teal = reported figure or lower endpoint. Hatching = range up to the upper endpoint. Approximate figures retain ≈. Imperial’s existing and further programmes are separate. No company-size adjustment.

Net workforce change — a different measure

Horizontal axis: percentage change from each company’s starting workforce. Different periods and definitions are shown; not annualised.

Teal = net growth; amber = net decline. Formula: (end − start) ÷ start × 100. Net movement combines hiring, departures and other changes. It cannot identify layoffs or their causes.

Sources, definitions and limitations

Ten deliberately selected companies, not a representative sample. Industry groups are editorial navigation categories, not pure business models: Amazon spans retail and cloud; Netflix is a media platform. FAANG means Meta, Apple, Amazon, Netflix and Alphabet/Google; NVIDIA is included alongside them.

Selected 2026 primary disclosures are compared as checked on 18 November 2026. Earlier rounds are labelled as earlier rounds. The selection is not an exhaustive history or a UK/European redundancy total; figures retain their reported company-wide scope. Source dates, reference dates and exit dates are different.

Three companies provide quantified reduction components in this selection. Netflix confirms reductions without a role count in the selected shareholder letter. Accenture and HSBC provide restructuring context; their workforce changes are not counted as layoffs. Apple, Alphabet and NVIDIA remain visible without a comparable reduction number. Missing is never zero.

Workforce comparison periods differ: Accenture is 12 months, TCS six months, HSBC nine months. Accenture inputs are approximate employees; HSBC uses employee FTEs and excludes contractors. HSBC’s baseline is derived from its reported endpoint and decline. No cross-company total, average rate or implied AI displacement estimate is calculated. Reported explanations are not independent proof of causation.
